# A few notes from the DM

Welcome, adventurers, to the world of Theia (_thay-uh_), a land where anything can happen, and has! This campaign is a journey into the dark, fantastical, and down-write degenerate mind of your DM. Expect high stakes, bizarre magical chaos, and the occasional existential monologue delivered by the most apathetic child you've ever met. You will be asked to confront world-ending crises that were caused by either bureaucratic oversight or divine incompetence, proving that the greatest tragedies are often just administrative errors writ large. Remember to pack your sharpest wit—and your most resilient filing system.

## The purpose of this site

This site aims to document some of the wider lore within the world of Theia, providing some context and flavour text around the place, people, and concepts that might be mentioned during the campaign.

Most of the content on this site has been written from an in-world perspective ands aims to present content as it would be by the travellers and scholers within Theia. Having said that, whilst the information presented within can be considered the generally accepted in-world truth, that does not mean it's completely factually correct. Public opinion is rarely rational or well-informed in any world, take the information on these pages with a pinch of salt.

In addition, knowledge revealed to the party throughout the campaign may not appear in these pages as it may not be considered widely-available information within the world. As information disseminates across Theia, the pages on this site may be updated, so watch this space!

## Character creation

To fit within the lore of the world, there are some restrictions to the character creation process you should be aware of (written below). For the most part you can follow the standard approach of character creation for the selected edition (see below); we'll be using a milestone based progression system, and hit points will increase by the fixed value for your class (rather than rolling each time). Also worth noting that the 2024 rules don't strictly list any "half-X" race, but if you want to pull these from the 2014 (original 5th edition) books then I'll allow that.

If you find any exotic races from any of the expansions online (such as those in [Mordenkainen Presents: Monsters of the Multiverse](https://dndstore.wizards.com/eu/en/product/813814/mordenkainen-presents-monsters-of-the-multiverse-digital-plus-physical-bundle)), and _really_ want to play one of those, talk to your DM and they may be able to work out a background that can fit into the world. No promises though.

* Every one starts at Level 1.
* Please use the 'Standard Array' method when assigning attributes.
* Don't go giving your character too grand of a backstory, no second comings of D&D-Jesus please.
* Valid races for your character (those marked with * can be selected, but must be discussed with the DM first):
  * Aasimar*
  * Dragonborn*
  * Dwarf
  * Elf (inc. Half-Elf)
  * Gnome
  * Goliath*
  * Halfling
  * Human
  * Orc (inc Half-Orc)
  * Tiefling*

Between the character creation session and the first real play session, the DM will ask you to go away and write some backstory for your character. This doesn't have to be long, but ideally will include some unsolved business or trauma that we can explore in the campaign. Of course, the DM may choose to tweak your backstory a little to better fit the world and their plans for the story, but they'll work on it with you.

## Edition & valid sources

I'll be running this campaign using the 2024 variant of edition 5 (which is basically just 5 with some tweaks). Unless provided by the DM during play, any spells, feats, or special skills will have to come from the core handbooks; Player's handbook, Dungeon Master's Guide, Monster Manual. Be aware however that your DRM may draw upon other sources of NPCs & Monsters to make the world just that little bit more chaotic.

There may come moments when, after specific story beats, your character is rewarded with a new spell, feat, or skill. These may be pulled from other sources, but a complete breakdown of the spell/feat/skill will be provided to you by the DM.


## Play style

These aren't quite specific enough to list as House Rules, but I'm going to list them here to make sure everyone knows what to expect in terms of play style and how I'll DM the campaign.

`Please keep meta gaming to a minimum:` There's nothing worse than a player that attempts to detect traps in every room their character walks into. Remember that if your character doesn't have a reason to be suspicious, neither should you!

`Remember to play your character:` Hopefully you've written you character with a particular personality in mind, play to that! Unless it was relayed by some in world dialogue, your character is not privy to the discussions you've had with your co-players. Having said that, if playing your character's personality is actively making the lession less fun for everyone else, the DM will likely ask you to _really consider_ what your character would do (*hint* *hint*).

`Good improvisation and roleplay will be rewarded:` I'm writing this campaign to lean towards a lighthearted whimisical style, although there will be more serious darker moments. Play to this and I'll likely reward you with advantage checks or inspiration.

`You won't have to worry about fungible goods:` Unless a specific story beat requires it, I'll not make players keep track of how much food or non-specialised ammo they have in their inventory. This also goes for material spell components worth less that 100GP.

## House rules

To make things a little spicer, I've decided to tweak a couple of the established rules and add a few more home-brew ones. Whilst I'll likely explain these as situations demand in the campaign, I wanted to list them here for prosperity; for ease of reference, I've given them all snazzy names as well.

`Hold your horses:` Rolling a natural 20 during an Initiative check lets you pick your position in the running order after hearing the other party member's order.

`Kip up:` Rolling a natural 20 on a death saving throw instantly revives the player with 1 HP.

`Heroe's end:` Any time a player is required to make a death saving throw, then can instead choose to take the Heroes end. Invoking this lets the character get back up immediately and take a single turn. During this turn you can do anything your character can normally do, all spell slots and abilities are refreshed and all rolls are at advantage. At the end of the turn your character dies, permanently and can't be resurrected in any way.

`The Jumanji rule:` No matter where the dice lands, if it's not cocked and you can retrieve it, you have to use the result.

`Bloodied:` Pulled from DnD 4e, it's a condition that every creature gets when it's below half maximum hit points. It's only use is for the DM to communicate how hurt the enemies are, they will not provide players with any enemies exact HP.
