# Beldar

Commonly called The Infinite Canvas or The Silent Void, Beldar is the literal distance between atoms and the yawning gulfs between galaxies. He is a passive, boundless entity that defines "where" things exist. Unlike the active forces of energy or gravity, Beldar does not act upon matter directly; rather, he provides the medium in which they interact. He is cold, silent, and unimaginably vast, seeking only to widen eternally, pushing the boundaries of existence outward into absolute nothingness.

In the cosmology of the Eight, Beldar is the prerequisite for existence. Before Ignis could burn or [Daesis](/info?content=daesis) could take form, there had to be a place for them to be. Beldar is that place. He is the emptiness that allows things to exist separate from one another. Without Beldar, the universe would be a single, undifferentiated point of infinite density - the ultimate goal of his cosmic rival, Oriana.

Beldar is described as a passive but relentless force. He is not aggressive, but he is expansive. His fundamental nature is to grow, stretching the distances between galaxies and widening the void. He is the silence between musical notes and the cold vacuum between stars. Philosophers argue that Beldar does not "do" anything, yet without him, nothing else could be done.

Despite representing emptiness, the canonical stained-glass depictions in the High Sanctum portray Beldar with immense presence, visualizing the concept of "encompassing vastness."

* **Appearance:** He is depicted as an older, overweight male figure. His massive frame represents the sheer scale of the universe; he is large because he contains everything.
* **Attributes:** He has a large, round face, symbolizing prosperity of space. His most striking feature is his long, flowing hair and beard, constructed from swirling segments of white, pale blue, and clear glass that resemble nebulae, cosmic dust clouds, and the milky way.
* **Attire:** His robes are composed of large, rounded geometric shards of translucent white and cyan glass, suggesting invisibility and formlessness. His eyes glow with a soft, cool white light, and he is surrounded by a halo of empty geometric patterns interspersed with sparse, distant stars.

## Worship and clergy

Clerics of Beldar are known as Voyagers or Void Gazers. They are explorers, cartographers, astronomers, and navigators; anyone whose livelihood depends on understanding and traversing great distances.

Worship is a quiet affair, often involving long periods of silence, meditation on the night sky, or the meticulous drawing of maps. The holiest act is to travel to a place where no one has ever been, expanding the known map of Beldar's domain. Beldar has no crowded city temples. His sacred spaces are high mountain observatories with open roofs, lonely lighthouses on the edge of great seas, or simple shrines located at the furthest frontiers of civilization.
