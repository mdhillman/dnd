# Revelstone

Revelstone, frequently referred to as The City of Gears and Glimmer, is a sovereign city-state and the administrative heart of the civilized world. Serving as the joint-seat of government for the allied nations, it acts as the de-facto capital of the Kindred Concord. Built directly atop the shattered foundations of the legendary city of Aethelgard, Revelstone is a sprawling metropolis where early industrialism and bleeding-edge magical research butt up against the polished remnants of a forgotten golden age. It is globally recognized as the premiere hub for magical research, technological innovation, and bureaucratic entanglement.

## History

### The Foundation of the Concord

Following the long stagnation following The Breaking, the various survivor nations realized that survival required more than just walls; it required a shared intellect. In the year 1170 of the New Age, the Kindred Concord was signed, and then then relatively small, independent city of Revelstone was designated as a "neutral ground of co-operation." Architects and mages worked in tandem to clear the remaining ruin, rubble, and splinters of shattered glass of Aethelgard to build a city that could house the collective future of the lower continent.

### The Great Steam-Burst (1218 NA)

A pivotal moment in the city’s development occurred when researchers attempted to tap into an ancient Aethelgardian "Mana-Well" located deep beneath the city. The resulting pressure caused a massive explosion of steam and raw magic that permanently altered the city's plumbing. Instead of a disaster, the engineers utilized the constant pressure to create the Great Central Boiler, which provides heat and power to the entire Central Quarter to this day.

## Governance

While each member nation of the Concord maintains its own domestic laws and leaders the overarching policy of the alliance is determined within the Halls of Consensus. Each country appoints three Council Persons to represent their interests. This Greater Revelstone Council (or GRC) is famous for its glacial pace of progress, its magnificent robes, and its occasional habit of accidentally legislating new colors into existence.

Within the walls of the Halls of Consensus, the 9 members of the GRC (three from each of the three Kindred nations) and their hundreds of attendents, accountants, and lesser clerks, attempt to steer the continent away from the precipice of magical or industrial disaster.

The Council is presided over by the High Arbiter, a spokesperson 'elected' by council vote to rule on meeting protocol, keep the minutes, and provide an amply supply of sweet treats during long debates. The High Arbiter is not elected by popular vote, nor is it a hereditary title. Instead, they are chosen by the "The Great Sorting" - a process where all nine councilors place their signet rings into a steam-powered brass urn. The urn, a relic from the early New Age, vibrates violently for several minutes before spitting out a single ring. It is widely believed that if the urn spits out two rings, the world will end.

### Rituals & superstitions

The Council is a hotbed of bizarre traditions that have been inherited from its consistuent countries, influenced by ancient Aethelgardian texts, or accumulated from the stupidity of the general populace over the last 250 years:

* The "Silent Minute": Before any vote, the entire Council must sit in absolute silence for sixty seconds. This is a superstitious carry-over from the Age of Legends, meant to allow the Octad to whisper guidance. In reality, it usually just results in twenty-four people awkwardly avoiding eye contact while listening to the building's pipes hiss.
* The Lead Ink-Pot: Every treaty signed in the Halls must be signed using ink kept in a lead-lined pot. This is to "weigh down" the words so they can't be stolen by roaming spirits or "erased" by the Department of Gaps.
* The Tea-Stain Augury: It is an unwritten rule that if High Arbiter spills his morning tea, the Council will not discuss any infrastructure of utility matters that day. The last time he spilled his tea, a boiler exploded in the Industrial District; now, the councilors watch his saucer with the intensity of hawks.
* The Empty Chair: There is a tenth chair at the table that remains empty at all times. Officially, it is for "The Traveler" (a very important spirit who's identity and purpose has long since been forgotten). Unofficially, it’s where everyone puts their coats, though they are careful never to sit in it for fear of being "spiritually misplaced."

## Security & Defense

### The Revelstone Militia

A well-disciplined force of volunteers and professionals who manage the day-to-day safety of the city. They are distinguishable by their polished brass breastplates and their "Boom-Sticks" which are kept meticulously clean, even if they are rarely fired.

### T.H.U.M.P. HQ

Revelstone serves as the primary base of operations for the Tactical Hex Unit & Magical Policing organisation. The headquarters, known as The Lead Vault, is a windowless fortress in the Industrial District where Commander Thaddeus Thorne monitors the city's magical "pollution" levels. The presence of T.H.U.M.P. ensures that the city’s high-energy research doesn't accidentally phase the population into a different dimension before lunchtime.

## Geography and Districts
Revelstone is a hectic mismatch of different physical elevations, commercial districts, cultural communities, enterainment empires, and varying levels of residential housing. All seperated by an interconnected mess of highways, byways, lanes, and down-right dangerous alleyways, navigating Revelstone is not a task to be taken lightly.

### The Central Quarter (The Glimmer)

The heart of the city, built on the highest remains of Aethelgard. This is the playground of the elite, where the streets are paved with "Resonant Cobbles" that glow softly at night.

* The Kindred Opera House: A massive structure of brass and velvet where Lyro Nox frequently performs to audiences of weeping aristocrats.
* The Grand Ballroom: Famous for hosting the Solstice Gala, where political alliances are made and broken over tiny cakes.
* The Halls of Consensus: Where the Great Revelstone Council meet to discuss the latest political maneuverings, tax brackets, and what the local canteen should serve for lunch next week. 

### The Technology Hub (Weavepool)

The academic and research hub. This district is populated by mages, engineers, and students from across the Concord. The sky here is often filled with the smell of ozone and the iridescent sparks of failed experiments.

* The Aethelgardian Archives: A library built into the hollowed-out base of an ancient tower, housing the world's largest collection of "Dead-Tech" schematics.
* The Glass Menagerie: A massive greenhouse-like structure built of "Resonant Glass" salvaged from the ruins below. It houses semi-sentient flora that still "pulses" with the light of the Age of Legends.
* Penumbra Observatory: A leaning, soot-stained tower that uses a massive, clockwork-driven lens to track the "Static Clouds" around the Spire.
* That area just off Rosehill Lane that's strangely colder than the surrounding area and always a little foggy. What's down there again? Just some kind of local park I think.

### The Industrial District (The Ironworks)

The industrial powerhouse where the city’s steam-engines and firearms are manufactured. It is loud, soot-covered, and incredibly productive. The workers here are known for their dry wit and their deep-seated distrust of anyone wearing a silk hat.

* The Copper-Crowning Foundry: The largest steam-casting plant in the Concord, famous for producing the massive brass pistons used in the city’s plumbing.
* The Dead-Tech Scrapyard:A sprawling, walled-off lot where Scavenger Guilds dump the unidentifiable chunks of machinery pulled from ancient ruins.
* Piston & Pipe Pub: A subterranean tavern built into a series of decommissioned steam-tunnels. It is the only place in Revelstone where a T.H.U.M.P. officer and a black-market artificer might share a pint of "Boiler-Ale".

## Culture and Society

Society in Revelstone is a study in contrasts. The elites of high society spend their days at theaters, indulging in the latest "Aether-Plays," or attending lavish balls where the fashion is dictated by the current color of the Spire’s glow. Meanwhile, the middle class consists of thousands of clerks and bureaucrats who keep the paperwork of the Concord moving.

The city is a magnet for celebrities, including:

* [Lyro Nox](info?content=lyro-nox): The undisputed "The Bard of Whispers" Lyro’s vocal range is so vast it has been known to accidentally shatter Resonant Glass and trigger the Spire’s glow.
* Master Artificer Bram: A man who famously replaced his own left arm with a steam-powered clock, claiming he "never liked the original's lack of built-in timing."
* Madame Valois: The most sought-after dressmaker in the Concord, rumored to weave "Anti-Memetic" thread into her garments so that her clients can attend scandals without being remembered by the witnesses.
* "Steam-Whistle" Sally: A legendary rail-pilot and engineer who famously drove a prototype locomotive through a Realmatic Flux storm to deliver medicine to a quarantined village in Doriath.
